\begin{abstract}
We compare MobileNetV2, ShuffleNetV2, and EfficientNet-B0 for poultry-fecal disease classification on resource-constrained edge devices. The audit of the published 8,770-image benchmark finds 617 near-duplicate pairs, including 94 test images byte-identical to training images; results therefore use an 8,153-image deduplicated split. Dynamic INT8 changes MobileNetV2 and ShuffleNetV2 only slightly but collapses EfficientNet-B0 to 10.42\%. Static per-channel QDQ recovers EfficientNet-B0 to 94.56\%, while selective ablation indicates strong sensitivity to depthwise-layer granularity. On Raspberry Pi~5, dynamic INT8 is 1.93--2.80$\times$ slower than FP32 at four threads, whereas static per-channel QDQ reaches 94.56\% at 13.50\,ms, 2.34$\times$ faster than FP32. These results show that deployment outcomes depend jointly on architecture, runtime, and quantization granularity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Poultry production is a major agricultural sector (combined broiler, egg, turkey, and chicken value of \$67.1~billion in the United States in 2023~\cite{usda2024}). Coccidiosis, Salmonellosis, and Newcastle Disease (NCD) cause major losses~\cite{machuve2022}. Fecal image analysis enables noninvasive early detection, but laboratory diagnosis remains inaccessible to many small farms~\cite{tasdelen2025}.

Recent deep learning approaches have achieved high accuracy for poultry fecal disease classification. Dhungana~\textit{et al.}~reported 99.12\% accuracy using EfficientNet-B0~\cite{dhungana2025}, while Tasdelen and Arslan achieved 97.1\% with MobileNetV2~\cite{tasdelen2025}. However, these solutions primarily target cloud-based deployment, leaving a critical gap: \textit{how can these models be efficiently deployed on resource-constrained edge devices at farm level?}

Lightweight CNNs---MobileNetV2~\cite{mobilenetv2}, ShuffleNetV2~\cite{shufflenetv2}, and EfficientNet~\cite{efficientnet}---are candidates for edge deployment because they need fewer operations. INT8 post-training quantization (PTQ) reduces size by using 8-bit weights~\cite{jacob2018}. Even on CPUs that expose INT8 acceleration, \emph{dynamic} PTQ can increase latency when the runtime kernel path adds quantize/dequantize work around every convolution; our x86 reference shows a 3.0--16.8$\times$ slowdown for dynamic INT8, whereas static QDQ is faster than FP32 on Raspberry Pi~5. Kernel-level attribution is discussed in Sec.~IV-C, and~\cite{nagel2021} explains why PTQ without calibration degrades on depthwise layers.

This paper addresses two research questions: (1) What is the quantization sensitivity of each lightweight architecture under INT8 PTQ on CPU, and where exactly does EfficientNet-B0 fail? (2) What is the accuracy-latency tradeoff for these models under INT8 PTQ on a Raspberry Pi~5? We additionally audit the dataset integrity claim underlying prior work on this benchmark.

The contributions are as follows. First, we audit the FecalFed leak-free claim~\cite{chi2026} and identify 617 near-duplicate pairs, including 94 test images byte-identical to training images; all results use the resulting deduplicated split. Second, we characterize EfficientNet-B0 sensitivity to quantization granularity: dynamic INT8 and static per-tensor QDQ collapse to 10.42\% and 9.32\%, while static per-channel QDQ recovers 94.56\%. Third, we characterize the three models on Raspberry Pi~5 across 12 model--quantization--thread configurations, showing that deployment latency depends on the runtime and quantization scheme.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Poultry Disease Detection}
Machuve~\textit{et al.}~introduced the foundational dataset of 6,812 PCR-verified fecal images from Tanzanian poultry farms~\cite{machuve2022}; subsequent work combined YOLO-based detection with CNN classification~\cite{dhungana2025,degu2023} or benchmarked architectures for accuracy and training speed~\cite{tasdelen2025}. Chi introduced FecalFed, a federated-learning benchmark on this dataset family claiming 8,770 rigorously deduplicated unique images~\cite{chi2026}; our audit in Section~III-A tests that claim directly. Prior work has not compared lightweight models under quantization with accuracy-latency tradeoff analysis.

\subsection{Lightweight CNN Architectures}
MobileNetV2~\cite{mobilenetv2} utilizes inverted residuals with linear bottlenecks and depthwise separable convolutions for efficient feature extraction. ShuffleNetV2~\cite{shufflenetv2} introduces channel shuffle operations for cross-channel information flow with only 1.26M parameters. EfficientNet~\cite{efficientnet} employs compound scaling to uniformly balance network depth, width, and resolution. Hoang~\textit{et al.}~recently compared seven lightweight architectures for plant disease detection, identifying hybrid designs as optimal for edge deployment~\cite{hoang2026}.

\subsection{Model Quantization}
INT8 quantization represents weights with 8-bit integers, theoretically achieving 4$\times$ compression~\cite{jacob2018}. ONNX Runtime notes that INT8 without matching kernel support may degrade performance~\cite{onnxruntime2024}, and~\cite{nagel2021} surveys why PTQ without calibration degrades on depthwise and SE layers.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Dataset}
We use the Machuve~\textit{et al.}~dataset~\cite{machuve22} with PCR-verified fecal images from Tanzanian farms, as distributed via HuggingFace~\texttt{Dianyo/poultry-fecal-fl}. Chi~\cite{chi2026} describes this benchmark as 8,770 rigorously deduplicated leak-free images with train/test splits of 7,016/1,754.

We audit that claim with pHash (Hamming $\le$ 5) over all 8,770 files and find 617 near-duplicate pairs (all size 2, class-consistent; 393 byte-exact), 303 of which span splits: 147 test--train, 54 test--validation, and 102 train--validation. Of these, 94 test images are byte-identical to a train image and 31 to a validation image.

Splitting precedes deduplication. The publisher train split (7,016) was carved into a stratified 80/20 train/validation partition (seed 42), with the publisher test split (1,754) intact; we then dropped one copy per near-duplicate pair (keeping test $>$ val $>$ train; within a split, lexicographically-first kept). This removed 513 train, 78 validation, and 26 test images, exactly 617, yielding 8,153 at 5,100/1,325/1,728 with zero cross-split survivors (artifacts \texttt{phash\_dedup.json}, \texttt{sterile\_split\_manifest.json}). Validation is 20.6\% of train plus validation rather than exactly 20\% because unequal numbers were removed from each side.

The partition contains no cross-split near-duplicate pair detected by this audit, which is not a claim of independence between images, since residual dependence may remain within a split. This removes leakage but does not rebuild a fresh split from the Zenodo originals (6,812 images); the test set remains 98\% inherited from the publisher test split. Classes are Coccidiosis, Healthy, Salmonella, and Newcastle Disease; NCD is underrepresented (417/5,100 train, 141/1,728 test). Images are resized to 224$\times$224 and normalized with ImageNet mean/std.

\subsection{Model Architectures}
We evaluate the three ImageNet~\cite{deng2009}-pretrained architectures of Sec.~II-B with the classification head replaced for four classes (3.4M$\rightarrow$2.2M for MobileNetV2 after the head swap). Our 4-class models hold 2.2M (MobileNetV2), 1.3M (ShuffleNetV2), and 4.0M (EfficientNet-B0) parameters.

\subsection{Training Protocol}
All layers are trainable (torchvision V1 weights, no freezing). Each model is trained once (seed 42) for 50 epochs with Adam ($10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) and cosine annealing, keeping the best-validation checkpoint; batch size 32, input $224\times224$. Augmentation (train only) is horizontal flip, $\pm 15^\circ$ rotation, and 0.2 color jitter; eval preprocessing is resize$\to$tensor$\to$ImageNet normalize. Training used an RTX 4060 (8\,GB) with PyTorch 2.5.1/CUDA 12.1.

\subsection{Quantization and Benchmarking (x86)}
Models are exported to ONNX (opset 13) and quantized with ONNX Runtime 1.29.0 dynamic per-tensor INT8 (signed QInt8 weights, dynamically quantized activations); per-channel scales use the output-channel axis. The static-QDQ run used the first 100 training images sequentially; because the dataset loader is class-ordered, this calibration set contained only Coccidiosis images and was not class-balanced, so the static-QDQ results are diagnostic evidence from a single-class calibration slice, not a claim of calibration robustness. Static QDQ uses signed QInt8 weights and activations, MinMax calibration, and per-tensor or per-channel weight scales, with no custom graph fusion. The native-Linux rerun, performed with the same ORT model-only protocol, gives dynamic-INT8 slowdowns of 14.73--14.97$\times$ for MobileNetV2, 3.22--3.26$\times$ for ShuffleNetV2, and 14.08$\times$ for EfficientNet-B0 at one and four threads, respectively. These host-reference values are reported separately from the WSL and Raspberry Pi~5 results.

\subsection{Edge Deployment on Raspberry Pi~5}
We characterize the same ONNX FP32 and INT8 models on a Raspberry Pi~5 (Broadcom BCM2712, 4$\times$ Cortex-A76 at up to 2.4\,GHz, 8\,GB LPDDR4X, Debian 13 trixie, kernel 6.18.34+rpt-rpi-2712, aarch64, \texttt{ondemand} governor, throttled=0x0). ONNX Runtime 1.29.0 is used with only \texttt{CPUExecutionProvider} (no NPU/GPU; \texttt{AzureExecutionProvider} listed but unused). The deduplicated-model sweep  covers 12 configurations: 3 models $\times$ 2 quantizations (FP32/INT8 dynamic) $\times$ 2 thread counts (1 and 4 via \texttt{intra\_op\_num\_threads}) $\times$ batch 1, with input 3$\times$224$\times$224. Each configuration runs 200 iterations after 10 warmup iterations (single session per config), reporting mean/std/P50/P95/P99 latency, throughput, RSS memory (mean/peak), CPU\%, and die temperature before/after. Variability reported is within-session; variability across independent benchmark sessions was not measured. All timings are model-only (eval preprocessing and data transfer excluded), not end-to-end application throughput. Current/power telemetry was unavailable (\texttt{current\_a=null}) on this board, so energy is discussed as a limitation. All 12/12 runs completed without thermal throttling.

EfficientNet-B0 has the highest FP32 accuracy (98.55\%) but collapses to 10.42\% under dynamic INT8; Fig.~\ref{fig:cm} shows 1,676/1,728 predictions falling in the Newcastle Disease column, with per-class INT8 recall of 2.27\% (Coccidiosis), 4.76\% (Healthy), 0.54\% (Salmonella), and 100\% (Newcastle Disease), i.e.\ a single-class predictor. Static per-tensor QDQ also collapses (9.32\%), whereas per-channel QDQ recovers 94.56\% (Table~\ref{tab:eb0static}). A stratified 25/class replication on a retrained model (same 50-epoch protocol, seed 42; FP32 98.15\%, 7 images below the main model) gives 27.14\% per-tensor and 93.40\% per-channel versus 9.32\% (coincidentally identical to the main model) and 90.74\% single-class on the same model: balanced calibration lifts per-tensor by 17.82\,pp but the collapse persists, while per-channel stays within 2.66\,pp of the single-class result, so the failure is not explained by calibration skew in this replication. A plausible mechanism is channel-wise range imbalance: a shared per-tensor scale is set by the largest channel, reducing resolution for smaller ones. A depthwise audit over 16, 17, and 19 layers gives median P95/P5 channel-range ratios of 39.94$\times$, 9.20$\times$, and 5.32$\times$ (\texttt{depthwise\_range\_audit.json}), and layer-level depthwise SQNR has medians of 16.67, 34.06, and 37.79\,dB (worst layer 13.87, 30.17, and 35.29\,dB), so EfficientNet-B0's depthwise family is uniformly worse rather than dominated by a few outliers. Its worst layer (480 channels, range ratio 112$\times$) has channels at 0\,dB, i.e.\ quantized to a constant, versus 12.23\,dB for MobileNetV2's worst channel. The family mean (27.97\,dB, Table~\ref{tab:sqnr}) hides this spread; we hypothesize that severely quantized channels drive the collapse, which a single-layer ablation would test. Section~\ref{sec:tradeoff} discusses the accuracy-latency implications.

\textbf{Measured bottleneck.} Table~\ref{tab:level2} quantifies why INT8 is slower. Dynamic PTQ does not insert static QuantizeLinear/DequantizeLinear nodes; it expands each Conv (52--81) into a DynamicQuantizeLinear + ConvInteger + Mul (scale) + Cast pattern, so node count grows by 1.37--3.05$\times$ on the deduplicated graphs. ORT kernel profiling on RPi5 (enable\_profiling, 15+5 runs) shows dispatched kernels: FP32 has zero quantization kernels, while INT8 dynamic dispatches ConvInteger 51--58\%, DynamicQuantizeLinear 9--15\%, and Mul/Add/Cast 20--35\% combined; all quantization-related kernels add 20.7--29.2\% overhead. The slowdown ranking agrees with the measured overhead ranking for these three models, but the sample is too small to claim a general expansion-overhead correlation. CPU feature detection exposes ARM dot-product support (\texttt{asimddp}) but not \texttt{i8mm} or SVE; whether the MLAS kernels use the dot-product instructions was not verified. Artifact \texttt{kernel\_dispatch\_artifact.json} contains the per-operator timings and operation counts, complementing TinyML system studies~\cite{david2021}.

\subsection{Accuracy-Latency Tradeoff}
\label{sec:tradeoff}

ShuffleNetV2 FP32 is fastest on RPi5 at 6.19\,ms, EfficientNet-B0 is most accurate at 98.55\%, and MobileNetV2 FP32 is intermediate (97.92\%, 14.23\,ms). The best and worst FP32 accuracies differ by only 0.86pp, so hardware constraints and quantization scheme should drive model choice. On x86, ShuffleNetV2 FP32 (4.15\,ms) is slower than MobileNetV2 (2.40\,ms) but faster on RPi5; its 902 graph nodes versus 170 (Table~\ref{tab:level2}) make per-node overhead a plausible, untested cause on the faster x86 core, so deployment claims rest on RPi5 only.


\subsection{Comparison with Related Work}

Our accuracy (98.55\%) is competitive with prior results on the same dataset family, although the protocols differ in splits, deduplication, and metrics, so this is not a head-to-head benchmark.

We do not claim depthwise sensitivity as a new finding: Sheng~\textit{et al.}~already showed separable convolutions quantize poorly~\cite{sheng2018}, per-channel weight quantization is the documented remedy~\cite{krishnamoorthi2018}, and cross-layer equalization~\cite{nagel2019} targets the same range imbalance we measure. Our contribution is narrower and empirical: a case study showing the failure appears in EfficientNet-B0 but not in MobileNetV2 or ShuffleNetV2 under an identical PTQ pipeline, plus the runtime behaviour of dynamic INT8 on ONNX Runtime for Cortex-A76 (1.93--2.80$\times$ slower than FP32 at four threads).

\section{Conclusion}

This paper characterizes lightweight CNN architectures with INT8 quantization for poultry fecal disease detection. Dynamic INT8 preserves MobileNetV2 and ShuffleNetV2 but collapses EfficientNet-B0 to 10.42\%; static per-channel QDQ recovers it to 94.56\%, and the ablation indicates strong sensitivity to depthwise-layer granularity (93.46\% with depthwise per-channel versus 8.33\% without). Dynamic INT8 is 3.0--16.8$\times$ slower on x86 and 1.93--2.80$\times$ slower on RPi5, whereas static per-channel QDQ is 2.34$\times$ faster than FP32 on RPi5.

This study covers one board, one runtime build, one dataset, one seed, and a single-class (Coccidiosis-only) 100-image calibration set; multi-seed intervals, calibration-size sweeps, and paired McNemar tests for the stratified balanced replication are absent from the reported tables. Energy was not measured and RPi5 latency is model-only at batch 1. The selective ablation covers EfficientNet-B0 only; extending it to MobileNetV2 and ShuffleNetV2 would test whether the depthwise sensitivity generalizes. Quantization-aware training and class-imbalance mitigation for the underrepresented NCD class remain open.
